## 1. A Tired Evening

After work, I am not always looking for pleasure. Sometimes I simply want to stop carrying things.

That is why games, short videos, food, shopping, and other forms of quick stimulation are so powerful. A game offers clear goals, immediate feedback, and a world where effort seems to have a visible result. After a day of fragmented attention and endless responsibilities, it can feel good to focus again.

The problem often appears after the screen goes dark.

The fatigue is still there. The emotions held back during the day have not disappeared. I may have felt focused, but not rested; entertained, but not restored. The empty space returns.

For a long time, I interpreted this as a planning problem. Perhaps I played for too long. Perhaps I did not study enough, read enough, or do enough to improve my life. Perhaps I needed a stricter evening schedule.

Now I think that conclusion came too quickly.

## 2. Repairing the Loop Is Not the Same as Changing Direction

There is a familiar loop: fatigue, immersion, emptiness, self-criticism, and a new plan to do better tomorrow.

The loop looks like a problem of time management. We ask how to play less, learn more, and make every evening feel more productive. But if free time becomes another performance review, then the pressure of the day has only extended into the night.

Our needs are often shaped by forces outside us. Work, comparison, social expectations, algorithms, and the desire for quick relief all pull on our attention. We still make choices, of course, but it becomes easy to forget whose life those choices are building.

Work, entertainment, consumption, and even self-improvement can become tasks we perform for someone else’s idea of a good life.

So perhaps the deeper question is not:

> How can I make tonight more meaningful?

It is:

> Where do I actually want to go, and did today bring me even a little closer?

## 3. A North Star Is Not Another To-Do List

I am not looking for one grand answer that can explain an entire life. I am also not looking for a slogan to put on the wall and use against myself.

I want something more like a north star.

A north star does not decide every step. It cannot remove fatigue, uncertainty, or desire. It only gives us a point for orientation. When we are lost, distracted, or pushed by other people’s expectations, it helps us ask whether we are still moving in a direction we can honestly call our own.

A real north star has at least three qualities.

First, it does not promise constant happiness. The things that matter most are often slow, repetitive, and sometimes difficult. Learning a language, training the body, or writing something honest may not feel rewarding every day. But they leave more than the brief excitement of stimulation.

Second, it can enter ordinary life. It affects how we use an evening, what we choose to read, how we treat fatigue, and which abilities we decide to build over time.

Third, it does not reject rest or entertainment. A north star is not a judge. It does not turn every game, every lazy evening, or every moment of escape into a moral failure. It simply refuses to let temporary needs become the captain of the whole journey.

## 4. A Less Popular Road

I am beginning to accept that I may want to walk a less popular road.

Not because being unusual is automatically valuable, and not because I want to define myself against the mainstream. But the default paths of constant busyness, comparison, consumption, and immediate feedback may not lead where I want to go.

The destination does not yet have a complete name. Still, I can see some of its outlines. I want to understand a wider world for myself. I want to have my own language and make my own work. I want to carry real responsibilities without becoming nothing more than a tool for completing tasks. In a time when technology is rapidly reshaping people, I want to keep independent judgment, a sense of beauty, and some inner quiet.

This road can begin with something concrete.

One of my next commitments is to build my English ability seriously. English is not only a test subject or a line on a résumé. It is a door to original sources, technical discussions, literature, and forms of expression that I cannot fully reach yet.

I may not speak smoothly at first. My listening may still be slow. But every conversation, every page, and every sentence I can finally understand expands the world a little. Learning a language is also a practice of agency: I do not only wait for other people to translate, summarize, and interpret the world for me. I gradually learn to enter it myself.

## 5. Let Action Become Evidence

A north star does not need to be perfectly defined before we begin. It may become visible only through repeated action.

A short English conversation today. A few pages read tomorrow. A paragraph written honestly on another night. A run that helps the body find its rhythm again. One difficult real-world problem handled with care.

None of these actions proves that a life is meaningful. But together, they can become evidence of direction.

Perhaps no one can completely escape being shaped by outside demands. We still need to work. We still get tired. Some evenings, simple pleasures will carry us away. But if we keep one point of orientation that is not entirely given to the world, then every detour does not have to become a defeat.

The goal is not to move in a perfectly straight line.

The goal is that when I look up again, I still know where I want to go.

I want to find that north star slowly. Not to appear different, but to be able to say, one day and without drama: this road may not be crowded, but it leads toward the place I truly wanted to reach.
